\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

% Research area and relevance
Representing three-dimensional shapes in computers is a fundamental challenge in graphics, robotics, and medical imaging. Rather than storing a grid of samples, a neural network can learn a continuous function that determines whether any point lies inside or outside an object, making memory cost independent of resolution. Standard networks struggle to capture sharp edges and fine detail, however, a limitation that has produced many specialized architectures and little guidance on which to choose.

% Aim and methods
This thesis evaluates eight such architectures under an identical training protocol on the Nefertiti bust, a cultural heritage scan combining smooth facial geometry with fine ornamental detail. We measure reconstruction quality by intersection-over-union on held-out points, alongside Chamfer distance and normal consistency, and test whether architectural properties proposed in a recent survey predict the differences observed.

% Results
Six of the eight architectures fall within 1.2 percentage points of one another, a margin indistinguishable from run-to-run variation given a single run per configuration, so we report the ordering without claiming it is meaningful. Two methods trail clearly, but fail to fit even their own training data, indicating an optimization difficulty rather than a representational limit. A controlled ablation on one method's frequency scale then moved accuracy by 16.7 points --- fourteen times the spread between architectures --- while improving training accuracy to a perfect score.

% Conclusion
Two conclusions follow. Configuration outweighs architecture selection here by more than an order of magnitude, inverting the emphasis of much of the surrounding literature. And the property-based grouping used to explain such comparisons is unreliable at this sample size: our data supports a 3.5 point advantage for frequency compactness, a 0.67 point disadvantage, or a 0.36 point advantage, depending on one hyperparameter or one included method. We quantify the repeated runs such conclusions require and release the implementation, dataset, and analysis scripts.
